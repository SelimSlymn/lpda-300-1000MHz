Cross-section

This section is taken perpendicular to the boom axis at an element position (here an odd-numbered element). The method is the same on both booms: the rod end sits on the boom face, and an M4×25 bolt entering from the opposite face screws into the M4 thread in the rod end. On odd-numbered elements the A arm points up and the B arm down; on even-numbered elements the other way round. The RG-316 runs along the upper outer corner of boom A; the bolts pass through the centre, so they do not clash. Scale approximately 6 px = 1 mm.

Cut list

Two rods of equal length are cut for every element, one for A and one for B. Cut length = h; the rod sits on the boom face, so this equals the simulated arm length. Direction: on A the odd-numbered elements point up and the even ones down; on B the opposite. Cut tolerance ±0.5 mm; take extra care with the shortest elements.

Fabrication steps

  1. Cut the booms. Cut both tubes to 1407.3 mm. Ends square, deburred. Write "A" and "B" on the booms with a permanent marker and mark the rear end (z = 0).
  2. Cut and number the rods. Following the cut list, cut two rods of length h for every element, 40 rods in total. Label them A01…A20, B01…B20 with tape.
  3. Square the rod ends. File the end of each rod that will sit on the boom, then square it on sandpaper fixed to glass. The element rests on the boom face with this end; if the end is skewed, the element will be too.
  4. Tap an axial M4 thread in the rods (D5).
    • With a lathe: face the end, centre-drill, drill Ø3.3 to 13 mm, and tap by hand with the tap supported by the tailstock.
    • Without a lathe, make a guide block: in a ~30 mm thick block, without moving it, drill Ø3.3 all the way through and then Ø10 to 22 mm depth from the same side. Hold the rod upright in the vice, slip the block over its end with the Ø10 side, and drill Ø3.3 through the guide hole 12–13 mm into the rod.
    • Put the tap in the drill chuck and, with the drill switched off, start it by hand for 2–3 turns; then continue with the tap wrench, one turn forward and half a turn back. Use cutting oil. Run the taper, intermediate and bottoming taps in order.
    • Check every rod by turning an M4×25 bolt fully in by hand.
  5. Mark the element positions. Clamp both booms side by side with their rear ends aligned. Fix the tape measure at z = 0 and draw all 20 positions across the top faces of both booms with a square in one go; centre-punch each line at the middle of the face. Take every measurement from z = 0, not from the previous hole.
  6. Drill the element holes (D1). Lay each boom in the vice on the drill press and check with a square that the drill is perpendicular. At every mark drill Ø4.5 through the top and bottom walls in one pass. 20 holes in each boom. Deburr.
  7. Prepare the spacers. Cut 6 POM blocks of 7.5×15×25 mm. Measure the thickness (gap) with calipers; it should be 7.5 ± 0.2 mm.
  8. Drill the spacer holes (D6). Clamp the two booms with the POM blocks between them at the correct z positions. At every spacer position drill Ø4.5 at mid-height through all four walls and the POM in one pass.
  9. Front-end holes (D7–D9). On the top face of boom A at z = 1395, 4 mm from the axis towards the outer edge, drill the Ø3.5 coax exit hole and deburr it carefully. Drill Ø5.0 holes at z = 1401 on the top face of boom A and at z = 1398 on the top face of boom B and set M3 rivet nuts.
  10. Shorting plate (D10). Cut a 37.5×15×3 aluminium plate and drill Ø3.2 holes near both ends, 22.5 mm apart centre to centre. On each boom, 7.5 mm in from the rear end, drill Ø3.2 through both walls at the centre of the top face.
  11. Mounting plate (D11). Drill Ø6.0 holes at z = 660 and 705 on the outer side face of boom B and set M4 rivet nuts. Drill Ø4.5 holes in the POM mounting plate at the same spacing and at z = 682.6 (spacer 3 bolt).
  12. Clean the contact surfaces. On anodized tube, sand to bare metal around the element holes (where the rod end sits) and where the plate and lugs sit. Also polish the threaded end faces of the rods.
  13. Join the booms. Insert the POM spacers, fit the nylon M4 bolts and tighten by hand. Measure the gap with calipers at several points. Multimeter: A to B must be open (∞ Ω).
  14. Pull the RG-316. Push a thin wire in from the open rear end of boom A and out through the Ø3.5 hole at the front, tie the coax to it and pull it through. Leave ~3 cm at the front and ~30 cm at the rear. Lay the cable in the upper outer corner.
  15. Fit the elements. Seat the threaded end of each rod over its hole: on A the odd-numbered elements on the top face and the even ones on the bottom face; on B the other way round. Pass an M4×25 bolt with washer from the opposite face and screw it into the rod. On boom A check that the coax stays in the corner before tightening. Hand-tight, do not overtighten. Check with a square that each element is perpendicular to the boom.
  16. Fit the shorting plate. Place the plate on the top faces of both booms at the rear end. Pass the M3×25 bolts through serrated washer, plate and boom, and tighten from below with a washer and nylon-insert nut. Multimeter: A to B is now a short circuit. This is normal for an LPDA.
  17. Connect the feed.
    • Strip ~10 mm of the jacket from the coax coming out of the Ø3.5 hole. Twist the braid into a single lead and solder it to an M3 ring lug; fix the lug with an M3×6 screw to the rivet nut on boom A (z = 1401).
    • Keep the PTFE insulation of the centre conductor up to 2–3 mm from the lug, solder its end to the second lug and fix the lug to the rivet nut on boom B (z = 1398).
    • Keep the exposed conductor ends shorter than 10 mm. Solder does not wet aluminium; solder only to the lugs.
    • Protect the joint from moisture with heat-shrink tubing and neutral-cure silicone.
  18. Fit the connector. Crimp an SMA or N connector onto the RG-316 at the rear end. Multimeter: you should see a short circuit between the centre pin and the body of the connector (centre conductor → boom B → plate → boom A → braid). This is correct.
  19. Fit the mounting plate. Bolt the POM plate to the rivet nuts on the outer side face of boom B with M4 bolts; the long nylon bolt of spacer 3 also passes through the plate. Attach the mast to the plate with a U-bolt. A metal mast must not be parallel to the elements: if the elements are vertical, offset the mast by at least 30–40 cm with a horizontal plastic arm, or rotate the antenna by 90° and use horizontal polarization.

Checks and measurement

  • Mechanical: All elements must be perpendicular to the booms and in one plane. The gap must be the same everywhere. No screw may protrude into the gap, and no metal part may bridge the two booms at the front.
  • Electrical (multimeter):
    • Before the plate is fitted, A–B must be open.
    • After the plate is fitted, A–B must be a short circuit.
    • Every rod must be shorted to its own boom and open to the other boom.
    • At the connector, centre pin to body must be a short circuit.
  • RF (NanoVNA): Calibrate at the end of the measurement cable and sweep 250–1100 MHz. Keep the front of the antenna clear, at least 2 m above the ground and away from metal. Compare the result with the S11 from CST.
    • If the measured curve is above the simulation across the whole band (poorer match), first shorten the feed leads.
    • If that is not enough, change the gap by 0.5 mm.
    • If the match is poor only at the top end (900–1000 MHz), the feed point and the connection lengths at the front end are the likely cause.

CST model

The CST model (cst/lpda.cst) matches this guide: d_el = 10, elements 21–22 removed, shortplate moved to the top face (Xrange -xA-a/2…xA+a/2, Yrange a/2…a/2+3, Zrange 0…15), gap = 7.5. Results are in results/final_20el_d10: lpda_20el_d10.s1p, the farfield tables and performance_table_d10.csv.

The dimensions in this guide are taken from the lpda.cst parameters: τ = 0.90, σ = 0.13, L1 = 555.2 mm, a = 15, gap = 7.5, stub = 138.8, front = 20, feed_back = −5, with elements 21 and 22 removed and d_el = 10.
